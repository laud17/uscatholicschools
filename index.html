Question 01

Who teaches in Catholic schools?

The typical Catholic-school teacher is experienced, and the workforce has become somewhat more diverse. The long-term age pattern also raises a continuing succession question.

45.9average age
15.0years of teaching experience
75.8%women
14.7%teachers of color

How the workforce changed

Older than it once was, with gradual gains in representation.

The share of Catholic teachers age 50 or older rose sharply in the early survey years. Since 2003–04, the shares of male teachers and teachers of color have increased, though both remain a minority of the workforce.

Age trend: 1987–88 to 1999–2000. Gender and race trends: 2003–04 to 2020–21.

Question 03

How satisfied are Catholic-school teachers?

Most Catholic teachers are satisfied with their school, but satisfaction with salary is much lower. Both measures were slightly lower in 2020–21 than in 2003–04.

Catholic school teachers, 2020–21

Commitment and pressure coexist.

More than nine in ten were generally satisfied at their school. Fewer than four in ten were satisfied with their salary, and nearly half said paperwork interfered with teaching.

Question 04

Do teachers feel supported by their schools?

Large majorities report supportive administration, useful materials, parent support, and cooperation among staff. Support for teaching students with special needs is notably less common.

Question 05

How much professional voice do teachers have?

Catholic teachers report substantial control over their classrooms, but far less influence over the institutional decisions around them.

Change over time

Classroom control remains high, but one measure fell.

Great control over student discipline fell from 73.6% in 2003–04 to 53.3% in 2020–21.

The autonomy split, 2020–21

Classroom authority does not extend to every school decision.

Teachers reported much more control over instruction than influence over hiring and budgets.

Methods and limitations

Data coverage

Which years can be compared?

The archive combines early pilot trends, validated estimates for 2003–04 through 2011–12, partial 2017–18 results, and a broad 2020–21 release. Measures are connected only when wording and response categories support comparison.

Read a trend carefully

A break may reflect a changed survey item or unavailable data. Early 1987–2000 estimates are provisional until their replicate-weight variance method is verified.

Methods

How should these estimates be interpreted?

The site reports weighted national survey estimates. It preserves standard errors, confidence intervals, and source flags when available. These data describe teachers and their working conditions. They do not establish that one working condition caused another outcome.
