National findings

A workforce defined by experience, commitment, and substantial demands on teachers’ time.

These estimates provide a first national portrait for the hub. They describe Catholic school teachers in 2020–21 and establish a foundation for the historical series to come.

01

Catholic school teachers report long workweeks.

Teachers reported an average of 50.3 hours of work per week, including 23.6 hours of instruction. Their reported total workweek exceeded those of teachers in other religious and nonsectarian private schools.

02

Nearly half plan to remain as long as they are able.

49.3% selected this response, while 18.3% were undecided. These are stated intentions, not observed turnover.

03

The workforce is experienced.

Catholic school teachers averaged 15 years of teaching experience and 45.9 years of age.

04

Pay differs considerably across private-school sectors.

The average Catholic school base salary was about $10,300 below the nonsectarian private-school average.

Research standards

Designed for use—and scrutiny.

Every published series should make its sources, definitions, uncertainty, and limitations visible.

Return to the data explorer →

Nationally weighted

Estimates use appropriate teacher weights and clearly identify the population represented.

Uncertainty visible

Future releases will carry standard errors, confidence intervals, suppression flags, and reliability warnings.

Trends harmonized

Question wording and category changes will be documented before estimates are compared over time.

Claims bounded

Cross-tabulations are described as relationships unless a research design supports causal inference.

Assistant Professor, Arizona State University

Lennon studies teacher labor markets, with a focus on the people and employment conditions shaping schools. This hub extends that research agenda to Catholic school teacher labor markets and is being developed as a durable public resource for researchers, educators, and policymakers.
